Short answer: yes. Tea and coffee count toward your daily fluid intake, and at normal amounts they don't dehydrate you. Water is still the best choice because it has no caffeine or sugar.

Why people think tea dehydrates you

Caffeine is a mild diuretic, so it can make you pee a bit more. But Mayo Clinic notes the effect is mild and the water in the drink more than makes up for it. A study in the British Journal of Nutrition found that black tea at typical amounts hydrated people about as well as the same amount of water.

How much caffeine is too much?

The FDA says 400 mg of caffeine a day is generally not associated with negative effects for healthy adults. Caffeine per cup varies a lot by tea type, coffee type and brew strength. If you feel jittery, get palpitations or can't sleep, cut back. Pregnant people have lower limits; ask your clinician.

What about energy drinks and juice?

They count as fluid too, but they add caffeine and sugar. Plain water remains the better default, especially in hot weather.

FAQ

Can I drink only tea instead of water?

It counts, but relying only on tea means more caffeine (and often more sugar). Keep water as your main drink.
