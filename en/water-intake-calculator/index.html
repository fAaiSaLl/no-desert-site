Water Intake Calculator: How Much Water Should You Drink a Day?

Last reviewed: 2026-11-24

Short answer: a common starting point is about 33 ml per kilogram of body weight a day (roughly half an ounce per pound), plus more for exercise and heat. For a 155 lb person with moderate activity that's about 2.7 liters (90 oz). Try the calculator:

Suggested daily goal—Enter your weight

A starting estimate you can adjust. Food also provides some of your water.

How the calculator works

It multiplies your weight in kilograms by 33 ml, then adds:

  • 350 ml (about 12 oz) for moderate activity, 700 ml (about 24 oz) if you work out most days.
  • 500 ml (about 17 oz) for hot weather (100°F / 38°C and above) or working outdoors.

This is a rule-of-thumb estimate, not a medical requirement. The National Academies' reference amounts that Mayo Clinic cites, about 3.7 liters (125 oz) for men and 2.7 liters (91 oz) for women, are total water from drinks and food combined, and food supplies roughly 20% of it. Needs vary with body size, activity, climate and health.

When you need more

  • Heat: for work in the heat, CDC/NIOSH advises about 1 cup (8 oz) every 15–20 minutes, and no more than 48 oz (1.5 quarts) per hour.
  • Exercise: drink before, during and after longer workouts.
  • Pregnancy and breastfeeding: needs go up; follow your clinician.

When to be careful

If you have kidney or heart disease or have been told to restrict fluids, follow your doctor, not a calculator. Drinking very large amounts quickly can cause low blood sodium (hyponatremia), which can be dangerous. A simple check: pale yellow urine and no thirst usually means you're doing fine.

FAQ

Does coffee or tea count?

Yes. Caffeinated drinks count toward your fluids, though water is still the best choice. See Does tea count as water intake?

Is "8 glasses a day" right for everyone?

It's an easy-to-remember goal, but some people need less and others more.
